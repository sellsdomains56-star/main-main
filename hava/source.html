In the lakes, forests and ancient stone of Sweden. From here, to tables around the world.

Why Sweden

Sweden is a land of ancient granite, deep pine forest and around 100,000 lakes. Rain and snowmelt sink slowly through the stone and come back up cold and clear.

100,000 Lakes

Sweden has nearly 100,000 lakes larger than one hectare.

69% Forest

Around 69 percent of Sweden's land is covered by forest.

2 Billion years

Most of Sweden's bedrock is between 1.65 and 2 billion years old.

Top 10 In the world

Sweden ranks in the world's top 10 for safe drinking water and sanitation.

The water

From sky to bottle

01

Rain and snow

Rain and winter snow fall on a quiet northern land of forest, lakes and open sky.

02

The forest

Around 69 percent of Sweden is forest. Moss, roots and pine needles slow the water down on its way into the ground.

03

The stone

Below lies some of the oldest bedrock in Europe: granite and gneiss, most of it up to two billion years old. Hard rock like this gives very little to the water, which is why water in Sweden is often soft and light.

04

The ice

When the last ice sheet melted, about 10,000 years ago, it left long ridges of sand and gravel called eskers. Water moves through them slowly and comes out clear. About half of Sweden's drinking water is groundwater, much of it from eskers.

05

The care

In Sweden, drinking water is regulated as a food. The Swedish Food Agency sets the rules for how it is tested and kept clean.
