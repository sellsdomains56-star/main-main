Could continued Iranian attacks on the Gulf trigger a Dubai property crash? If fewer people move here, businesses delay expansion, and new homes keep entering the market, prices could come under serious pressure. But the evidence doesn't show every business pulling back. Some global investment firms have continued with regional expansion plans despite the conflict. Others have taken a more cautious approach. Their presence supports business activity. It doesn't guarantee property prices. Dubai ended {2025|twenty twenty five} with approximately {4.58|four point five eight} million residents, after adding around {332,000|three hundred thirty two thousand} people. By September {2026,|twenty twenty six} the reported population had reached around {4.8|four point eight} million. The counting methodology has changed too, so you can't treat every increase as a newly arrived resident or assume the same growth continues indefinitely. Investor interest has also held up. In an April survey commissioned by Arada and conducted by Penta Group, {56%|fifty six percent} of {689|six hundred eighty nine} property investors expressed serious interest in UAE real estate. That was ahead of the US and UK among the markets surveyed. But interest isn't a completed purchase, and population growth won't support every project equally. Watch actual sales, rents, business expansion, and completed supply. Uncertainty can create opportunities. Before buying, check whether the property still works if rents soften, handover is delayed, or selling takes longer than expected. Buy because the numbers work, not because someone tells you Dubai can't fall.
