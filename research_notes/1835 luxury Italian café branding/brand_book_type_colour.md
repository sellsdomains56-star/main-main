# Brand book contents, typefaces and colour directions for "1835" (luxury Italian-inspired café)

Researcher note on method and reliability: searches were run on 2026-11-23. Several foundry pages (Typotheque Parmigiano, Commercial Type Chiswick) returned 404 to the fetch tool, and Pantone's site did not return hex values, so many prices and all Pantone codes could not be verified at source. Everything under "Cited Findings" has a link. Anything I know from background knowledge but could not source in this session is placed under "Inferences" or "Gaps" and marked UNVERIFIED. No Pantone code in this file is an official brand specification unless stated.

## 1. Brand book contents (standard sections, real models)

### Takeaway
No single canonical luxury-café guideline PDF was found; published hospitality manuals converge on a common spine (brand promise, logo and lock-ups, clear space, minimum size, misuse, colour, typography, photography, applications), and agency case studies (Mucca, Pentagram) show what the application layer looks like for cafés: monogram plus logotype rolled across menus, cups, matchbooks, coasters, signage, uniforms and website.

### Cited Findings
- One hotel manual's table of contents runs: Logo, Lock-ups, Typography, Colour palette, Editorial, Applications. A restaurant manual (Whitegrass) runs: Brand promise, logo reverse, clear space, minimum size, incorrect usage, colour theory, typefaces, then food / drink / restaurant photography. (Summarised from search results; mostly sample and student manuals, treat as structural examples only.) — [Issuu hotel manual](https://issuu.com/candiceallen211/docs/allen_art333_brandingmanual); [Scribd No18 Brand Style Guide](https://www.scribd.com/document/551170273/No18-Brand-Style-Guide)
- Clear-space rules are normally tied to a letter in the logo: Jasper's Cafe uses the height of the "J"; Orchard Garden Hotel uses half the height of the "O"; a Hong Kong restaurant uses 0.5x the logotype height; Bonne Vie uses the cap height of "X" on all sides. — [Jasper's Cafe](https://issuu.com/oh18magazine/docs/jasper_s_cafe_lounge_brand_guidelines); [Orchard Garden Hotel style guide](https://www.scribd.com/document/216375949/OGH-StyleGuide); [Hong Kong brand guidelines](https://www.scribd.com/document/470529740/Brand-Guidelines-Hong-kong); [Bonne Vie](https://issuu.com/clandreth/docs/bonne_vie_brand_manual_pages/s/23791437)
- Misuse pages typically show: cropping, warping, altering colours, reducing opacity, overlaying on graphics that intrude on clear space. — [Jose Loco's Brand Guide](https://issuu.com/morgan_gullette/docs/gullette-jose_loco_brand_guide)
- Hotel manuals list signage types one by one (parking, room, touchscreen wayfinding, restroom, restaurant signage); café guides cover takeout packaging, staff uniforms and storefront signage. — [Application manual on Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/700235803/ApplicationManual)
- A hotel brand guide should carry through to website, social accounts, email marketing, signage and product packaging. — [Hotelchamp](https://hotelchamp.com/blog/craft-strong-hotel-brand-guide)
- Mucca, Saraghina Caffè (Brooklyn): custom display typeface "Saraghina Condensed" drawn from Italian poster and hand-lettering artists; monogram and logotype used across menus, cups, matchbooks, signage and routed into millwork. — [World Brand Design](https://worldbranddesign.com/?p=269633); [Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/47398/saraghina-caffe)
- Mucca, Cafe d'Avignon (NYC): logo, environmental graphics, packaging, menus and website, with commissioned illustration (Jeffrey Fisher); system built to flex across store sizes. — [Dexigner](https://www.dexigner.com/news/33864); [PRINT](https://www.printmag.com/branding-identity-design/mucca-serves-old-world-tradition-to-nyc-with-charming-identity-for-cafe-d-avignon/)
- Mucca, Manhatta: menus, coasters, wayfinding and a custom paperclip monogram as a "surprise detail". — [World Brand Design](https://worldbranddesign.com/?p=195101)
- Pentagram, Tigermilk (Paris): logotype whose overscored M forms a negative-space T, which becomes a "TM" monogram for menus, coasters and clothing. — [Pentagram](https://pentagram.com/work/tigermilk/story)
- Pentagram, La Pecora Bianca (NYC): animal motif plus wordmark in Futura Passata with handwritten "Caffé e Cucina" tagline. — [Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/la-pecora-bianca)
- Pentagram, Parla (café): custom wordmark with 1960s-70s Italian-inspired graphics; Halcyon and Area as brand type. — [Pentagram](https://pentagram.com/work/parla)
- Pentagram, Super Peach: a family of hand-drawn wordmarks used interchangeably on menus, stationery, coasters, uniforms. — [Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/super-peach?rel=sector&rel-id=6)
- Sant Ambroeus (Milanese café group): identity described as two custom typefaces giving a 1930s flavour to menus and packaging, with a rich pink and gold palette. — [Behance write-up](https://behance.net/gallery/7143041/Sant-Ambrus); signature pink confirmed by [Highsnobiety](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/sant-ambroeus-interview-collaboration-release-info/)
- Marchesi 1824 (Prada Group): brand identity described by the owner as "iconic packaging featuring pastel shades and gold leaf finishing". — [Prada Group](https://pradagroup.com/en/brands/marchesi.html)

### Inferences
- Recommended brand-book section order for 1835, assembled from the structures above plus standard practice (the list itself is my synthesis, not a quoted source):
  1. Brand story (why "1835"), positioning, audience, values, personality
  2. Tone of voice (English and Arabic), naming and menu-writing rules, vocabulary do/don't
  3. Logo suite: primary wordmark, numeral monogram, crest/seal, secondary and horizontal lock-ups, favicon/app icon; clear space, minimum sizes, colourways, misuse
  4. Colour: primary, secondary, accent, neutrals; Pantone C and U, CMYK, RGB, hex; foil and paper specifications; proportion rules (for example 60/30/10)
  5. Typography: display, text, sans, Arabic; numeral style rules (lining vs oldstyle, tabular for prices); hierarchy; tracking values; web fallbacks
  6. Pattern, ornament, borders, engraving or illustration style, iconography
  7. Photography and art direction (product, interior, people, hands, still life), colour grade, AI image-generation prompt rules
  8. Applications: stationery, menus, packaging, uniforms, signage and wayfinding, social templates, website, motion and sound
  9. Retail line (beans, tins, gift boxes), co-branding and partner rules
  10. File index, licence register (fonts, images), contacts, approval process
- The agency models suggest the most "ownable" asset for a numeral-named café is the monogram (as with Tigermilk's TM and Manhatta's paperclip): "1835" is itself a four-figure lockup, so the numerals of the chosen typeface are the logo. Typeface selection should therefore be judged on figures first.

### Gaps
- I did not locate a publicly published, complete guidelines PDF from a real luxury café or five-star hotel brand (Hilton's identity system was referenced in results but not opened). The cited manuals are mainly student or small-business documents, useful for structure, not as quality benchmarks.
- No Here Design, Base Design, Studio Frith or Leone Design case study was retrieved; the search surfaced only Mucca and Pentagram. Brand New has a podcast episode with Mucca on the Tin Building ([transcript PDF](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/wp/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/BN_Podcast_Transcript_Ep_062.pdf)) which I did not read.
- No sourced model for motion guidelines or sonic identity in café brand books.

## 2. Full list of deliverables (touchpoints) at opening

### Takeaway
Sources document the categories (menus, cups, coasters, matchbooks, packaging, uniforms, storefront and wayfinding signage, website, social) but none gives one complete checklist; the consolidated list below is my synthesis and should be treated as a planning checklist, not a cited standard.

### Cited Findings
- Documented café/restaurant applications in agency work: menus, cups, matchbooks, signage, millwork (Saraghina); environmental graphics, packaging, menus, website (Cafe d'Avignon); menus, coasters, wayfinding (Manhatta); menus, coasters, clothing (Tigermilk); menus, stationery, coasters, uniforms (Super Peach). — [World Brand Design](https://worldbranddesign.com/?p=269633); [Dexigner](https://www.dexigner.com/news/33864); [World Brand Design](https://worldbranddesign.com/?p=195101); [Pentagram](https://pentagram.com/work/tigermilk/story); [Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/super-peach?rel=sector&rel-id=6)
- A restaurant exterior typically carries signage with logo, typography and brand colours. — [VistaPrint](https://www.vistaprint.com/hub/signage-for-restaurants)

### Inferences
Consolidated opening checklist (synthesis, UNVERIFIED as a standard):
- Identity masters: wordmark, monogram, crest/seal, lock-ups, pattern, icon set, in vector plus font licences
- Stationery: business cards, letterhead, envelopes, compliment slip, wax or embossing seal, stickers and tape, gift card and holder, loyalty card, invoice and receipt templates, email signature
- Menus: main menu, coffee and bean list, dessert/pastry cards, seasonal inserts, bilingual English/Arabic versions, allergen sheet, QR menu, menu board, bill folder, table numbers, reserved signs
- Takeaway packaging: hot cups (three sizes), lids, sleeves, cold cups, carriers, paper bags (two sizes), pastry boxes, cake boxes, greaseproof and tissue paper, napkins, stirrers, sugar sachets, stickers and seals, ribbon
- Tableware and in-house: cups and saucers with backstamp, glassware, coasters, trays, matchbooks, napkin bands, placemats
- Retail: bean bags (250 g / 1 kg), bean tins, capsule or drip boxes if relevant, labels with roast date and origin, gift boxes, shipping cartons, hang tags, care or brew guide insert
- People: aprons, shirts or jackets, name badges, pins
- Space: fascia sign, blade/projecting sign, window gilding or vinyl, door handle and opening hours, wayfinding, restroom signs, neon or illuminated element, awnings, A-board, hoarding for pre-opening, delivery vehicle livery
- Digital: website (cinematic landing page, menu, shop, story, reservations), favicon and social avatars, Instagram/TikTok templates (posts, stories, reels covers, highlights), Google Business profile assets, email newsletter template, delivery-app listing imagery, Wi-Fi splash page, playlist cover
- Motion: logo animation, title cards, lower thirds, loading animation, launch film
- Launch: invitation, press kit, opening-night collateral

### Gaps
- No source gave quantities, sizes or supplier specifications for Dubai. Arabic-language signage requirements for Dubai trade names were not researched (out of scope here but relevant to the logo suite).

## 3. Typefaces (serif/Didone, sans, script) with foundry, licence, price, free alternative, Arabic companion

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced route to a bilingual Italian-luxury system is a contemporary Bodoni for Latin (Parmigiano by Typotheque for a paid system, or Bodoni Moda as the free OFL fallback) paired with a high-contrast Arabic from 29LT (Zarid Display or Azahar Display), since no Bodoni family with a native Arabic was confirmed. Prices below are only those I could see on a page; everything else is flagged as unverified.

### Cited Findings

Didone / serif display
- Parmigiano (Typotheque): third-party listings show Parmigiano Text Pro as 12 styles, with Fontstand rental at about €12/month per style or €64/month for the family (desktop and web, ex VAT). The family includes Text, Caption, Headline and Sans cuts. — [Fontstand](https://fontstand.com/fonts/parmigiano-text-pro); [Typotheque Parmigiano Sans](https://www.typotheque.com/fonts/parmigiano_sans)
- Bodoni Moda (indestructible type, Owen Earl): free, SIL Open Font License 1.1, commercial use allowed; variable with weight and optical-size axes; has italics and a small-caps companion (Bodoni Moda SC). The sister "Bodoni*" project lists eight optical sizes (6, 11, 16, 24, 36, 48, 72, 96). — [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Bodoni+Moda/about); [indestructible type](https://indestructible-type.github.io/Bodoni.html); [GitHub](https://github.com/indestructible-type/Bodoni)
- Libre Bodoni: second free Bodoni on Google Fonts. — [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Libre+Bodoni/about)
- Canela (Commercial Type; designed by Miguel Reyes; debuted 2014 in Document Journal): flared display face between sans and serif, begun as an interpretation of Caslon, stone-carving influence; 12 styles (Thin to Black with italics). Retailer type.today lists $72 per style and $468 family for desktop, the same for web, and $108 / $700 for app. — [type.today](https://type.today/en/canela)
- Klim Type Foundry pricing model (since 4 Oct 2021): separate desktop, web and app licences; $60 minimum per single font; desktop licence is per user and explicitly allows creating a trademark with the fonts; web licence covers websites, dynamic web ads and email marketing. — [Klim](https://klim.co.nz/blog/changes-to-eulas-new-pricing/)
- Ogg (Sharp Type, New York, founded 2015): calligraphic display serif; also listed on MyFonts under Monotype distribution. No price captured. — [Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/ogg-font/); [MyFonts](https://myfonts.com/collections/ogg-display-font-monotype-imaging)
- Saol Display (Schick Toikka, released 2017). No price captured. — [Typewolf](https://typewolf.com/saol-display)
- GT Sectra (Grilli Type, foundry founded 2009). No price captured. — [Grilli Type](https://www.grillitype.com/about)
- Editorial New (Pangram Pangram): foundry fonts are free to trial for personal, non-commercial use; commercial use needs a paid licence, and a separate Social Media licence is required for commercial social use. Third-party guides quote "from $30 per family" and "from $40 per font" (conflicting, unverified at source). — [Pangram Pangram FAQ](https://pangrampangram.com/faq); [licenseorg](https://licenseorg.com/guide/fonts/pangram-pangram); [Listicler](https://listicler.com/blog/pangram-pangram-pricing-deep-dive-design-studios)

Sans companions

- Brandon Grotesque (HVD Fonts): 12 styles from $40 on Fontspring. — [Fontspring](https://www.fontspring.com/license/desktop)
- Avenir Next Arabic (Linotype/Monotype): styles from $79, complete family of 10 fonts $314.99 on MyFonts; this gives Avenir a native Arabic companion. Avenir Next World is the pan-script superfamily. — [MyFonts Avenir Next Arabic](https://www.myfonts.com/fonts/linotype/avenir-next-arabic); [MyFonts Avenir Next World](https://www.myfonts.com/fonts/linotype/avenir-next-world)

- 29LT Ada Sharp is another high-contrast display style (Ruqʿah-based). — [TDC](https://tdc.org/winner/29lt-ada-sharp-flat-and-round)
- Greta Arabic / Greta Text Arabic (TPTQ Arabic, Typotheque's Arabic sister foundry; Kristyan Sarkis, 2015): Naskh-influenced; Greta Text Arabic is the high-contrast counterpart, usable at display sizes. TPTQ Arabic gives 10% volume discount on orders of €1,000-1,500. — [TPTQ Arabic](https://tptq-arabic.com/fonts/greta_text_arabic/about); [volume discounts](https://tptq-arabic.com/help/ordering/volume_discounts)
- Lyon Arabic (Commercial Type, 2020; Khajag Apelian and Wael Morcos): text family drawn to match Lyon Text, drawing on Naskh and Nastaliq; a display cut was announced for 2021 (release not confirmed here). — [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/wael-morcos-khajag-apelian-lyon-arabic-commercial-type-graphic-design-300720); [AIGA Eye on Design](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/one-design-two-flavors-lyon-arabic-brings-a-new-slant-to-traditional-characters/)

### Inferences
- Because the name is numerals, shortlist by figures: Didones give the strongest "1", "8", "3", "5" (hairline flag on the 1, ball terminals on 3 and 5, stacked-bowl 8). Test each candidate by setting "1835" at logo size and at 6 mm (cup base stamp) before judging the alphabet. A drawn/customised numeral set is likely (as Mucca did for Saraghina).
- Suggested systems (my recommendation, not sourced):
  - Paid, heritage-Italian: Parmigiano (display + text + caption optical sizes) with Semplicità-family sans (Semplicita Pro via Adobe Fonts is the cheapest legal route if an Adobe subscription exists) and 29LT Zarid Display or Azahar for Arabic.
  - Softer, more sensual: Canela (flared, less clichéd than Bodoni) or Ogg / Saol for headlines, with the same Arabic.
  - Zero-licence fallback for web and AI mock-ups: Bodoni Moda (OFL) + a Google sans. UNVERIFIED suggestions for free pairings from my own knowledge: Jost or Josefin Sans as Futura-like sans; Cormorant Garamond and Playfair Display as free display serifs; Noto Naskh Arabic or Amiri as free Arabic. None of these were checked in this session.
- Klim's explicit "trademark allowed under desktop licence" clause is not universal. Logo, social media and broadcast use are separate licences at some foundries (Pangram Pangram is confirmed to require a separate social licence). Budget for: desktop (designers), web (pageview-tiered), social/video where required, and app if a loyalty app follows.
- Rough budget ESTIMATE for a three-family licensed system (display serif, sans, Arabic) for a small team, desktop + web: roughly US$1,000-2,500, extrapolated from the listed figures above (Canela $468 + $468; Futura Now $460-576; Zarid €175). Treat as an estimate.

### Gaps
- Could not verify at source (pages 404 or not fetched): Typotheque's own Parmigiano prices, designers, script coverage and whether it has an Arabic; Commercial Type direct pricing for Canela and anything on Chiswick; prices for Ogg, Saol, GT Sectra; anything on Romana, ITC Bodoni, Bauer Bodoni, Didot cuts, Neutraface, Gill Sans Nova, Forma DJR, Fortuna, Cormorant, Playfair, Noto Naskh.
- UNVERIFIED background knowledge the writer may want checked before use: Parmigiano was released around 2013-2014 for the Bodoni bicentenary, designed by Riccardo Olocco and Jonathan Pierini, with wide script coverage; Chiswick is by Paul Barnes (Commercial Type) and based on British vernacular lettering with extensive swashes; Forma DJR is David Jonathan Ross's revival of Nebiolo's Forma; Bauer Bodoni is commonly regarded as the most delicate metal-era Bodoni; "Bodoni Egyptian" is a monoline slab reinterpretation (Shinn Type) and not a Didone. None of these statements has a link in this file.
- No engraved/script typefaces were researched (for example copperplate scripts or engraver's faces for the crest and menus). This is a real hole for the "engraving" direction.
- I found no Didone with a designed-together Arabic of the same name; pairing is by contrast and proportion, and should be tested by an Arabic-literate designer.

## 4. Colour: Italian luxury and sensuality palettes

### Takeaway
Reference shades can be named and culturally justified, but almost none have an official public Pantone or hex: Campari, Marchesi and Savoy blue all lack a published brand specification, so 1835 must define its own values against a physical Pantone book. Pink (Sant Ambroeus) and pastel green (Marchesi/Prada) are already owned by Milanese café brands; deep red, bottle green and black-plus-brass are freer but generic unless tied to one distinctive accent.

### Cited Findings
- Campari red: no official public hex. Circulating values (#B8141B on ColourLovers; a brighter #C8102E elsewhere) are approximations; Pantone 186 C is commonly rendered as #C8102E and 185 C as #E4002B on third-party reference sites which state their values may not match official Pantone. — [Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/campari-brand-guidelines/); [pantonecolors.net](https://pantonecolors.net/ko/colors/red/)
- Marchesi 1824 green: when Prada reopened the Milan shop it kept the original green scheme; the pale/minty green became synonymous with Prada and was reused for Prada Caffè at Harrods with green velvet upholstery; packaging is pastel with gold leaf finishing. No official hex or Pantone found. — [Robb Report](https://robbreport.com.my/style/the-sweet-side-of-fashion/); [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2023/04/11/prada-caffe-patisserie-restaurant-bar-harrods-london-interiors/); [Prada Group](https://pradagroup.com/en/brands/marchesi.html)
- Sant Ambroeus: signature pink scheme across its restaurants; pink and gold palette in the identity; a 2026 trade item mentions "burgundy branding" (conflict unresolved). — [Highsnobiety](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/sant-ambroeus-interview-collaboration-release-info/); [Behance](https://behance.net/gallery/7143041/Sant-Ambrus); [pulse.bot summary of WWD](https://www.pulse.bot/food/news/sant-ambroeus-fetes-90-years-during-milan-design-week-gears-up-for-beverly-hills-debut-c5e652ba-988f-4559-b1c8-fea219fbd8f2/)
- Savoy blue (azzurro Savoia): most-cited hex #4B61D1 (RGB 75, 97, 209); colour of the House of Savoy, Italy's royal dynasty 1861-1946, and origin of the national sporting "azzurro" (first worn by the football team 6 January 1911). No single official value; other cited variants include #007CC3 (Piedmont Region label). — [Wikipedia: Savoy blue](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy_blue)
- Named reds with hex values per Wikipedia: Carmine #960018; Cardinal #C51E3A (named after cardinals' cassocks); Maroon #800000; Cinnabar #E44D2E. — [Wikipedia: Shades of red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shades_of_red)
- Metallics: Pantone 871 C-877 C are the classic gold and silver metallic inks; final appearance depends on stock and coating and must be judged on the target paper. Metallic ink is subtler on uncoated paper; hot foil is a physical film so it stays consistent across substrates. Foil reads as most luxurious on uncoated and cotton stocks where the matte surface makes the shine a deliberate accent; use 80 lb cover or heavier because light stocks buckle under the die. — [Print Peppermint](https://printpeppermint.com/gold-foil-stamping-vs-pantone-metallic-inks-vs-metallic-foil-paper-vs-cold-foil-vs-scodix); [Pantone](https://www.pantone.com/articles/product-spotlight/importance-of-metallics-in-print-and-packaging); [Printing for Less](https://www.printingforless.com/resources/hot-foil-stamping/)

### Inferences
- Candidate directions for 1835 (my proposals; hex values below are working ESTIMATES for screen mock-ups only, NOT sourced and NOT Pantone matches; final values must be picked from a physical Pantone Solid Coated/Uncoated guide):
  - "Rosso notte": oxblood/bordeaux primary (est. #4A0E17 to #5C1A1B), ivory paper (est. #F3EBDD), brass foil, black lacquer accent. Most directly "sensual"; closest to the brief.
  - "Verde bottiglia": bottle/racing green (est. #0F3B2E), cream, brass, with a Campari-type red (near 186 C family) as the 10% accent. Reads Italian bar/aperitivo; risk of resembling generic heritage green cafés.
  - "Nero e ottone": black lacquer and espresso brown with brass and a single saturated red or Savoy-blue accent. Most "Dubai luxury", least distinctive.
- Cultural associations (general knowledge, UNVERIFIED in this session): Pompeian red links to Roman frescoes; Venetian red and sienna are earth pigments tied to Italian painting and architecture; "giallo Milano" is the ochre-yellow of Milanese façades; rosso corsa is Italy's motor-racing red; bottle green and brass evoke historic bars. Use these as story hooks only after verifying.
- Over-use: in café branding generally, kraft brown + black, and cream + forest green + gold, are the saturated territory (judgement, unsourced). Within Milanese-luxury café codes specifically, pink is taken by Sant Ambroeus and pastel green by Marchesi/Prada (sourced above), so both would read as derivative.
- Foil and paper guidance (synthesis): specify foil by supplier swatch rather than by Pantone metallic number; define a "brass" (warmer, less yellow than standard gold) and require a press proof on the actual uncoated stock; keep foil to marks and numerals, not body text; pair matte uncoated or soft-touch surfaces with one gloss element (foil, spot UV, lacquered tin) because the matte/gloss contrast is what reads as sensual.

### Gaps
- No official Pantone/hex found for: Campari, Marchesi green, Cova, Bulgari, rosso corsa, British racing green, oxblood, bordeaux, Venetian red, Pompeian red, sienna, giallo Milano. The Wikipedia red page was only half-read (Venetian red and vermilion entries not reached). Pantone's own site did not return values to the fetch tool.
- "Cova pink" could not be confirmed at all; no result described Cova's brand colours.
- No Kurz/Luxor foil references or GF Smith Colorplan paper names were confirmed; ask suppliers for swatch books.
- No market data on which colours are over-used in café branding; the statement above is designer judgement.

## 5. Logo systems and 2025-2026 luxury trends; what reads as "sexy"

### Takeaway
Agency precedent supports a multi-part system (wordmark, monogram, motif, interchangeable secondary marks) rather than a single logo, and 2026 commentary points away from pure quiet luxury toward craft plus colour, sensuality and "editorial serifs with personality", with heritage used as source material and not costume.

### Cited Findings
- Systems in practice: logotype + monogram applied across every surface (Saraghina); logotype that yields a monogram (Tigermilk TM); motif + wordmark + handwritten tagline (La Pecora Bianca); a family of interchangeable wordmarks (Super Peach); hidden small-scale monogram details (Manhatta). — [World Brand Design](https://worldbranddesign.com/?p=269633); [Pentagram](https://pentagram.com/work/tigermilk/story); [Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/la-pecora-bianca); [Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/super-peach?rel=sector&rel-id=6); [World Brand Design](https://worldbranddesign.com/?p=195101)

## 6. Photography / art-direction references and AI image briefs

### Takeaway
The sourced references give a usable vocabulary: Newton for hard, sculptural, cinematic light and dominant subjects; Roversi for soft, studio, Polaroid/large-format intimacy on plain backdrops; Testino/Ford Gucci for stark, voyeuristic glamour. AI brand briefs are typically built as a reusable style snippet plus style-reference images, with short subject prompts.

### Cited Findings
- Helmut Newton: odd angles, dramatic lighting and erotic poses portraying dominant women; film influences (Riefenstahl, von Stroheim) and Brassaï, Erich Salomon; hard light used for sculptural, hard-edged results. — [The Art Story](https://theartstory.org/artist/newton-helmut/); [iGuzzini](https://cdn2.iguzzini.com/lighthinking-photography/100-years-of-helmut-newton)
- Paolo Roversi: known for Polaroid and large-format film; prefers working in his studio over location; simple backdrops to remove distraction. — [Thames & Hudson](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/blogs/all-news-features/into-the-studio-with-paolo-roversi); [Pace Gallery](https://www.pacegallery.com/artists/paolo-roversi)

- AI briefs: build a reusable "style snippet" from existing brand assets and append it to every prompt. — [Moodboard newsletter](https://moodboard.beehiiv.com/p/a-prompt-for-ai-images-that-match-your-brand-style)

### Inferences
- Suggested art-direction split: "day" imagery in a Roversi register (soft window light, plain plaster or velvet backdrop, shallow focus, slight motion blur, warm ivory highlights) for product and pastry; "night" imagery in a Newton/Testino register (hard single source, deep shadow, glossy black and brass, hands and partial figures) for brand campaign and website hero.
- AI brief template for the brand book (synthesis): fixed style block [medium and film stock; lens and aperture; light source and direction; palette in brand colour names plus hex; surface materials; grain; crop rule; mood words] + variable subject line + negative list (no visible logos or text, no extra fingers, no plastic sheen, no stock-photo smiles) + 3-6 approved style-reference images + aspect ratios per channel. Keep generated lettering out of images and composite the real logo afterwards. Naming living photographers in prompts raises style-imitation and rights concerns; describe the technique instead (my caution, not sourced).
- For Dubai, consider modesty norms in people imagery: the "sensual" register can be carried by objects, hands, texture, light and copy more safely than by nudity-adjacent fashion references (judgement, not researched).
