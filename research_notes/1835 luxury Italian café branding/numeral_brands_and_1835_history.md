# Numeral-named brands and the history of the year 1835 (fact file for the "1835" luxury café brand)

Research date: 23 November 2026. Evidence labels used throughout: **[STRONG]** = confirmed by a primary or several independent sources; **[MEDIUM]** = single secondary source or Wikipedia only; **[WEAK]** = secondary description, conflicting sources, or a tenuous link to the brand. Anything I could not source is in "Gaps", not in "Cited Findings".

## A1. How do year/number-named brands treat numerals in their identity?

### Takeaway
The sourced examples show three recurring patterns: the number becomes the whole name once it is famous enough (Kronenbourg 1664 → "1664 Bière"; 4711; Chanel's standalone "5"), the number is a sub-line to a word (N°5 above CHANEL; "Marchesi 1824"), and the number's meaning is always explained by one short origin sentence (house number, founding year, first barrel year, building year). I could not find reliable design case studies naming the typefaces of most of the brands on the list, so the typographic detail (lining vs old-style, serif vs sans) is largely a gap that needs visual inspection of the actual logos.

### Cited Findings

**Kronenbourg 1664 / 1664 (beer)**
- [MEDIUM] Carré Noir redesigned the logo; the rebrand "straightened and strengthened the line work on the lettering and added a gold extruded outline" — [Packaging of the World](https://packagingoftheworld.com/?p=8654)
- [WEAK, secondary logo site] The 1664 numerals are enlarged and take the central part of a silver-framed white oval; an older version set the year in italic bold numerals with gold strokes — [logos-world.net](https://logos-world.net/kronenbourg-1664-logo/)
- [STRONG] In 2024 the UK product was rebranded "1664 Bière" and the Kronenbourg name was dropped from the front of pack, i.e. the numerals alone now carry the brand — [Food Manufacture](https://www.foodmanufacture.co.uk/Article/2024/04/08/kronenbourg-name-scrapped-and-rebranded-as-1664); [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/cmbc-downplays-kronenbourg-name-in-1664-revamp/690080.article)

**Chanel N°5**
- [MEDIUM] The 1921 label used a simple sans serif with only "Nº5, CHANEL, PARIS" on it — [Grafik](https://www.grafik.net/fifth-element)
- [WEAK, secondary logo site] Logo described as minimalist monochrome uppercase sans serif with a small "N°5" above the word CHANEL — [whatthelogo.com](https://whatthelogo.com/logo/chanel-no-5/45846)
- [MEDIUM] Chanel filed a trademark application in 2021 for a stylised number 5 on its own, and uses "5" as a standalone motif in fine jewellery — [The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/with-new-trademark-application-chanel-is-looking-to-register-the-number-5/); [Chanel](https://www.chanel.com/gb/fine-jewellery/number-5/)

**4711**
- [STRONG] The name is the house number assigned to the Glockengasse building in Cologne during the French occupation (brand says 1794; a historical marker says 1796) — [4711 FAQ](https://4711.com/en/pages/faq); [4711 meaning page](https://4711.com/en/pages/bedeutung-4711); conflicting year in [HMDB](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=264725)
- [MEDIUM] The number was registered as a trademark in 1875 — [Deutsches Historisches Museum blog](https://dhm.de/blog/?p=1013); see also [German Patent and Trade Mark Office (DPMA)](https://www.dpma.de/en/explore/milestones/brands-with-history/worlds-oldest-perfume-manufacturer)
- [WEAK] One retailer source says the modern logo was created in 1875 and finalised in 1900 and unchanged since; a German source dates the number on bottles to 1881 — [Smallflower](https://smallflower.com/blogs/news/4711-first-eau-de-cologne); [Planet Wissen](https://www.planet-wissen.de/gesellschaft/mode/parfum_lust_auf_duft/pwiewissensfrage176.html)

**1800 Tequila**
- [STRONG for the brand claim] Name comes from the year the Beckmann family says tequila was first placed in barrels — [1800 Tequila "Our Story"](https://www.1800tequila.com/our-story/)
- [MEDIUM] Launched as "Cuervo 1800" (1967 per Wikipedia; 1975 per Luxe Digital, conflicting) and later shortened to the number alone — [Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/1800_Tequila); [Luxe Digital](https://luxe.digital/brands/1800-tequila/)
- [MEDIUM] Packaging redesign by Stranger & Stranger as part of a brand relaunch — [Stranger & Stranger](https://strangerandstranger.com/work/1800-tequila); [The Spirits Business](https://www.thespiritsbusiness.com/?p=783963)

**Marchesi 1824 (Prada Group)**
- [STRONG] Trades as "Marchesi 1824" (word + founding year); packaging described by the owner as "pastel shades and gold leaf finishing" — [Prada Group](https://pradagroup.com/en/brands/marchesi.html); [Marchesi history](https://www.marchesi1824.com/en/history)
- [MEDIUM] Staff uniforms carry "the italic Marchesi logo" embroidered on the shirt front — [WWD via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/first-look-italian-historic-caf-223000276.html)

**Cova**
- [STRONG] Uses "Tradition since 1817" / "Since 1817" as a tag under the name rather than putting the year in the name — [Pasticceria Cova](https://pasticceriacova.com); [Cova history](https://pasticceriacova.com/en/pages/storia)

**1883 Maison Routin**
- [MEDIUM] Maison Routin relaunched its professional syrup line "1883" with a new logo and fully redesigned bottles — [FoodBev](https://www.foodbev.com/?p=44401)

**THE1835, Rosewood Vienna (directly relevant precedent)**
- [STRONG] A luxury hotel bar that already uses this exact number; written as one closed-up token "THE1835" / "The1835"; "Named after the year this historic building was erected" — [Rosewood Vienna](https://www.rosewoodhotels.com/en/vienna/experiences/an-evenings-aperitif-at-the1835)

### Inferences
- The number-only route is earned over time: 1664 and 1800 both started with a word attached (Kronenbourg, Cuervo) and dropped it later. A new brand called only "1835" skips that stage, so the logo itself has to do the work a word normally does.
- Every example pairs the number with a one-line explanation. A numeral brand without an origin sentence reads as an address or a date. The owner's own meaning for 1835 is therefore the key missing input.
- Italian heritage cafés (Cova, Marchesi) use the year as a claim of age ("since/dal"). A new café using "1835" as a name, and especially "Dal 1835" or "Est. 1835", would imply it was founded then. That is a misleading-claim risk (consumer protection and trademark deceptiveness) unless the year is clearly framed as something else.

### Gaps
- No reliable source found naming the typefaces or figure style (lining vs old-style) for Marchesi 1824, Cova, Caffè Florian, 1800, 4711, Maison Francis Kurkdjian 724, Carolina Herrera 212, 1883 Routin, Byredo, Aesop, Bar 1802, Gucci 1921, or Cartier/Bulgari year references. Searches returned nothing usable; Fonts In Use was not successfully queried for these. These need direct visual inspection of the logos.
- No sourced examples found of split/stacked year layouts (18|35) or year monograms; nothing found on "Eighteen Thirty"-style spelled-out restaurant names.
- 1000logos.net (Kronenbourg history) returned HTTP 403, so the dated sequence of 1664 logo changes is unverified.
- Origin of "724" (Maison Francis Kurkdjian) and "212" (Carolina Herrera) not verified in this session.

## A2. Which typefaces are known for especially beautiful numerals?

### Takeaway
I found sourced general guidance on figure styles but no authoritative source ranking the specific faces asked about (Didot, Bodoni, Canela, Caslon, Chronicle Display, Romana, Ogg). The usable sourced points are about old-style versus lining figures and a few named display faces.

### Cited Findings
- [MEDIUM] By the nineteenth century old-style figures were largely displaced by lining figures because of newspapers and advertising; so lining figures are the period-correct style for an 1830s reference — [typetype.org](https://typetype.org/blog/numbers-and-numerals-in-typography-basic-types/)
- [MEDIUM] Old-style figures are recommended for running text and are "particularly beautiful when set in extra-large sizes" — [CreativePro, "dot-font: Numbers Don't Lie"](https://creativepro.com/?p=121006)
- [MEDIUM] Old-style figures were traditionally tied to serif faces but are now widely available in sans serifs; OpenType fonts commonly offer lining/old-style and tabular/proportional sets — [Type Network, "OpenType at Work: Figure Styles"](https://typenetwork.com/articles/opentype-at-work-figure-styles)
- [MEDIUM] Named faces praised for figures: Mantinia (Matthew Carter), Elysium (Michael Gills), and Worthe Numerals (Ben Kiel, House Industries), the last praised as the most expressive forms in the fat-face genre — [Adobe Typekit blog, "By the numbers"](https://blog.typekit.com/2017/12/20/by-the-numbers-great-type-for-numerals/); [Typographica](https://typographica.org/?p=9894)
- [WEAK, forum opinion] TypeDrawers "Favourite Figures?" thread mentions ITC Modern and "hybrid" (three-quarter height) figures as designer favourites — [TypeDrawers](https://typedrawers.com/discussion/comment/31133)

### Inferences
- Worthe Numerals is a fat-face numeral set, and fat faces are an 1800s-1830s style (see B4), so it is a period-relevant candidate worth a look alongside Didones.
- In "1835" the digits 1, 8, 3, 5 differ sharply between figure styles: in old-style figures the 3 and 5 descend below the baseline and the 1 is x-height, giving an uneven, calligraphic silhouette; in lining figures all four align, which suits a monogram, seal or stacked 18/35 layout. This is standard typographic knowledge but is my own reasoning, not a sourced claim.

### Gaps
- No citable source found for the numerals of Didot, Bodoni, Canela, Caslon, Chronicle Display, Romana or Ogg specifically. The writer should treat any statement about those faces as designer judgement to be checked against foundry specimens (Commercial Type for Canela, Hoefler&Co for Chronicle/Didot, Sharp Type for Ogg).

## A3. Trademark position for a purely numeric name, and existing "1835" brands

### Takeaway
Numerals are registrable in the EU in principle (stated in the Regulation and confirmed by the Court of Justice), with descriptiveness and lack of distinctiveness as the obstacles; I could not verify the exact UAE/GCC statutory wording in this session. "1835" is not free of prior use in hospitality: there is a luxury hotel bar (THE1835, Rosewood Vienna), a café-restaurant in the Galápagos (1835 Restaurant & Coffee / "1835 Coffee Lab") and a dead US spirits application. I found no venue called 1835 in the Gulf, but no official register was searched.

### Cited Findings

**EU**
- [STRONG] In C-51/10 P (Agencja Wydawnicza Technopol v OHIM, the "1000" case) the Court of Justice held that a sign made up exclusively of numerals is not for that reason barred from registration; Article 4 of the Regulation lists numerals among registrable signs. A numeral is refused where it describes a characteristic of the goods/services (e.g. a quantity) — [Fieldfisher](https://fieldfisher.com/en/insights/1000-a-round-number-but-still-descriptive); [Pinsent Masons Out-Law](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/numbers-can-be-trade-marks-but-not-when-descriptive-rules-ecj); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/trademark/1586792/can-you-register-a-number-as-a-trademark-in-the-eu)
- [STRONG] Earlier registrations of other numeral marks do not bind the Office; each application is examined on the rules in force — same sources as above
- [MEDIUM] In 2023 the EUIPO First Board of Appeal refused a figurative mark made of the numbers 0 to 23 for lack of distinctiveness (consumers see number sequences as codes) — [IPKat](https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2023/05/euipo-board-of-appeal-holds-numeric.html?m=0)

**UAE**
- [MEDIUM] The governing law is Federal Decree-Law No. 36 of 2021 on Trademarks; Article 2 defines a trademark and Article 3 lists non-registrable signs. A search excerpt of Article 2 lists "names, words, signatures, letters, figures, graphics, logos, titles, hallmarks, seals, pictures, patterns, announcements, packs or any other marks or group of marks" — official text at [UAE Legislation portal](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1535/download); [WIPO Lex entry](https://wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/21302)
- [MEDIUM] The 2021 law also extends protection to non-traditional signs (3D, sound, smell, holograms) — [Lexis Middle East](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/legislativeInsight/UnitedArabEmirates/UAE_Trademark_Law/en)

### Inferences
- "1835" for a café is not descriptive of coffee or café services (it is not a quantity, price, size or grade), so the EU descriptiveness objection that sank "1000" is unlikely to apply directly. The realistic risks are (a) weak inherent distinctiveness of bare digits in plain type, which argues for filing a stylised/figurative mark and/or a combination such as "1835 + word", and (b) the deceptiveness point if the mark is presented as a founding date.
- The Rosewood Vienna bar is in the same class of services (bar/restaurant, class 43) and the same luxury tier. Whether Rosewood holds registrations for THE1835, and in which territories, should be checked before any EU/Austria expansion.
- The Galápagos café shows that "1835 + coffee" already exists as a combination, anchored to Darwin. It is geographically remote from the Gulf but matters for domain names, social handles and search results.

### Gaps
- **Not verified:** the exact official English wording of UAE Article 2 and whether it uses the word "numbers" (the excerpt found says "figures"; the PDF could not be opened and u.ae and a law-firm summary returned errors). The GCC Trademark Law wording was not checked at all. A UAE trademark agent should confirm.
- **No register searches were run.** WIPO Global Brand Database, EUIPO eSearch, USPTO (beyond the one Justia hit) and the UAE Ministry of Economy database are interactive tools I could not query. A professional clearance search in classes 30, 35 and 43 is essential; the list above is only what open web search surfaced.
- Spelled-out "Eighteen Thirty-Five" brands: none found, but not systematically searched.
- Fashion and wine uses of 1835 were not found, but the search was shallow.

## B1. Coffee history around 1835

### Takeaway
There is no single famous coffee event dated exactly 1835 that I could verify. The honest framing is that the 1830s were a turning point: the vacuum (siphon) brewer appeared, Brazil rose to about 30% of world output, and Mocha was in political turmoil and decline. All of these are decade-level links, not year-level.

### Cited Findings
- [MEDIUM, decade-level] The earliest known vacuum (siphon) coffee maker design is credited to Loeff of Berlin in the 1830s; Madame Vassieux of Lyon patented the twin-globe form in 1841; James Robert Napier's vacuum machine dates to 1840. Some sources give earlier dates for a Nörrenberg device (1823/1827), so the origin is contested — [Wikipedia, Vacuum coffee maker](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Siphon_coffee); [Hario, History of the Syphon](https://www.hario.co.uk/blogs/news/history-of-the-syphon); [Google Arts & Culture, "Scientific brewing"](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/qQWhX2rN84rQKQ)
- [MEDIUM, decade-level] By the 1830s coffee was Brazil's largest export and Brazil accounted for about 30% of world production; it became the world's largest producer in the 1840s to about 1852 depending on source — [Wikipedia, Coffee production in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Brazil)
- [MEDIUM] Mocha remained a major coffee-exporting port until the early 19th century and was then eclipsed by Aden and al-Hudaydah — [Britannica, Mocha](https://www.britannica.com/place/Mocha-Yemen); [Wikipedia, Mokha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokha)
- [MEDIUM] In the 1830s Mocha was politically unstable: US diplomat Edmund Roberts, visiting in the 1830s, noted it was held by Turkish "rebels"; British records refer to a prohibition by Ibrahim Pasha, commander of the Egyptian army in Yemen, on selling coffee at Mocha to British merchants — [Wikipedia, Mokha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokha); [Qatar Digital Library record](https://qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100023859738.0x00008f); background in [American Institute for Yemeni Studies, "Mocha trade in the early 19th century"](https://www.aiys.org/post/mocha-trade-in-the-early-19th-century)
- [STRONG, near-miss years] Cafés and confectioners founded close to but not in 1835: Caffè Pedrocchi, Padua, inaugurated 1831 — [Caffè Pedrocchi](https://caffepedrocchi.it/en/history); Confiserie Sprüngli, Zurich, 1836 — [Sprüngli milestones](https://www.spruengli.ch/images/spruengli-welt/medien/informationen-zur-confiserie-spruengli/milestones-confiserie-spruengli.pdf); Pasticceria La Bomboniera, Trieste, 1836 — [Discover Trieste](https://www.discover-trieste.it/discover/the-city-of-coffee/historical-cafes/pasticceria-la-bomboniera)

### Inferences
- Link strength to the brand: WEAK to MEDIUM. "1835, the decade the siphon was born and coffee went global" is defensible only if worded at decade level. Claiming a specific coffee invention in 1835 would be invented.

### Gaps
- No café, roaster or coffee brand verifiably founded in 1835 was found (English and Italian searches).
- No espresso or percolator patent dated 1835 was found; I did not verify percolator patent history.
- No 1830s figures for Java output or for Mocha export volumes were found.

## B2. Italy in 1835

### Takeaway
The strongest Italian 1835 story is operatic: Bellini's last opera and Donizetti's most famous opera both premiered that year, and Bellini died three days before the Lucia premiere. It is verifiable to the day, Italian, and luxurious in tone, but its link to coffee is only atmospheric (opera-house café culture, e.g. Cova beside La Scala).

### Cited Findings
- [STRONG] Bellini's *I puritani* premiered 24 January 1835 at the Théâtre-Italien, Paris (cast included Giulia Grisi, Rubini, Tamburini, Lablache) — [Wikipedia, I puritani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_puritani); [Interlude](https://interlude.hk/?p=151537)
- [STRONG] Donizetti's *Lucia di Lammermoor* premiered 26 September 1835 at the Teatro San Carlo, Naples, with Fanny Tacchinardi Persiani in the title role — [Royal Opera House Collections](https://www.rohcollections.org.uk/work.aspx?work=521); [Wikipedia](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Lucia_di_Lammermoor); [Met Opera timeline](https://metopera.org/discover/education/educator-guides/lucia-di-lammermoor/composer-timeline)
- [STRONG] Vincenzo Bellini died 23 September 1835 — [Wikipedia, 1835](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1835)
- [MEDIUM] Italians born in 1835: astronomer Giovanni Schiaparelli (14 March), poet and later Nobel laureate Giosuè Carducci (27 July), Cesare Lombroso (6 November) — [Wikipedia, 1835](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1835)
- [STRONG] Caffè Cova was founded in 1817 by Antonio Cova, a former Napoleonic soldier, beside La Scala, and served as a meeting point for Risorgimento patriots; it moved to Via Montenapoleone only in 1950 — [Cova history](https://pasticceriacova.com/en/pages/storia); [Wikipedia, Caffè Cova](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caff%C3%A8_Cova)
- [STRONG] Caffè Pedrocchi in Padua (architect Giuseppe Jappelli, opened 1831) was the "café without doors", open day and night from 1831 to 1916, and a site of the 1848 student uprising against the Austrians — [Caffè Pedrocchi](https://caffepedrocchi.it/en/history); [Wikipedia, Pedrocchi Café](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Pedrocchi_Caf%C3%A9)
- [MEDIUM] Marchesi was founded in Milan in 1824 — [Marchesi 1824 history](https://www.marchesi1824.com/en/history)

### Inferences
- In 1835 Milan's Cova (18 years old), Marchesi (11) and Padua's Pedrocchi (4) were all already open, so "1835" sits squarely inside the golden age of the Italian grand café. This is a true contextual claim and a MEDIUM-strength anchor: Italian and café-related, but about the era, not the year.
- The opera story is STRONG on facts, WEAK on coffee, STRONG on Italian luxury mood. Note that the *Puritani* premiere was in Paris, not Italy.

### Gaps
- No Italian café, pasticceria, roaster, distiller or chocolatier founded in 1835 was found ("dal 1835"/"fondata nel 1835" searches returned only other years: Caflisch 1825, Baratti & Milano 1858, etc.).
- Nothing sourced on Italian art, design or typefaces specific to 1835, or on Turin/Venice/Naples café life in that exact year.

## B3. The Gulf in 1835

### Takeaway
This is the strongest and most locally meaningful 1835 story: on 21 August 1835 the rulers of Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Sharjah/Ras Al Khaimah and Ajman signed the first maritime truce, covering the pearling season. Its annual renewals led to the name "Trucial States", the forerunner of the UAE. It is thoroughly documented but has no direct connection to coffee or Italy, and it is a British-brokered colonial-era agreement, which needs sensitive handling.

### Cited Findings
- [STRONG] The truce was signed at Bushire on 21 August 1835 by four rulers: Sultan bin Saqr Al Qasimi (Sharjah and Ras Al Khaimah), Shakhbut bin Dhiyab Al Nahyan (Abu Dhabi), Obeid bin Said bin Rashid (Dubai) and Rashid bin Humaid Al Nuaimi (Ajman). It applied from May to November 1835, i.e. the pearling season — [Wikipedia, Perpetual Maritime Truce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpetual_Maritime_Truce); primary account in [Lorimer's Gazetteer of the Persian Gulf, Qatar Digital Library](https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100023575945.0x000027)
- [STRONG] It was proposed by Samuel Hennell, British Political Resident, after a first meeting at Basidu on Qeshm island; purpose was peace at sea during the economically vital pearling season — [Wikipedia, Perpetual Maritime Truce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpetual_Maritime_Truce); [Wikipedia, Samuel Hennell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Hennell)
- [MEDIUM, conflicting] One encyclopaedia says the ruler of Sharjah and Ras Al Khaimah himself first suggested that the British oversee a truce — [Encyclopedia.com, Treaty of Maritime Peace in Perpetuity](https://encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/maritime-peace-perpetuity-treaty-1853)
- [STRONG] The truce was renewed annually, became year-round from 1838, was followed by a ten-year truce on 1 June 1843 and the Perpetual Maritime Truce on 4 May 1853; the signatories became known as the "Trucial" sheikhdoms/States — [Wikipedia, Perpetual Maritime Truce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpetual_Maritime_Truce); [GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/gulf/uae-history-british.htm)
- [MEDIUM] Dubai context: in 1833 about 800 members of the Al Bu Falasah (Bani Yas) migrated from Abu Dhabi to Dubai under Maktoum bin Butti and Obeid bin Said, during the pearling season; Dubai then had about 250 houses around Shindagha and Al Fahidi Fort. Obeid, who agreed to the 1835 truce for Dubai, died in 1836 and Maktoum ruled alone — [Wikipedia, Maktoum bin Butti bin Suhail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maktoum_bin_Butti_bin_Suhail); [Wikipedia, History of Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Dubai)
- [MEDIUM] Coffee route into the Gulf: a 19th-century account describes Muscat's ships trading to Mocha, "from whence they bring the coffee of Yemen" — [Qatar Digital Library](https://www.qdl.qa/archive/81055/vdc_100119016907.0x000032)

### Inferences
- Link strength: Gulf/UAE = STRONG (exact date, named rulers of Abu Dhabi and Dubai, two years after the Al Maktoum arrived in Dubai). Coffee = WEAK (no source ties the truce to coffee; Yemeni coffee reached the Gulf by dhow via Muscat in this era, and a peaceful sea would have protected all trade, but that connection is my inference). Italy = NONE.
- A possible bridging narrative is "1835: the year the sea was made safe for the pearl season at home, and the year of *Lucia* in Naples", two true events in the same year (truce 21 August, *Lucia* 26 September). This is a constructed pairing, not a historical connection, and should be presented as such.
- Sensitivity: the truce is part of the history of British control in the Gulf and uses the period language of "piracy". UAE cultural bodies' own framing should be checked before building public messaging on it.

### Gaps
- No source found specifically on gahwa customs, pearl-trade values or coffee import volumes in the Trucial sheikhdoms in 1835.
- I did not open the Lorimer page text itself; details rest on Wikipedia's summary plus the search excerpt of the Qatar Digital Library record.

## B4. Other resonant 1835 facts (science, luxury houses, typography)

### Takeaway
1835 has several well-documented "wonder" stories (Halley's Comet, the Great Moon Hoax, the first photographic negative, Darwin in the Galápagos, Andersen's first fairy tales) but few luxury houses: the only spirits/luxury name tied to 1835 is Courvoisier, and that date is disputed by the brand itself. In type history the 1830s are the decade of fat faces, Didones and the first sans serifs with lowercase.

### Cited Findings
- [STRONG] Halley's Comet reached perihelion on 16 November 1835; Mark Twain was born two weeks later, 30 November 1835 — [Wikipedia, 1835](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1835)
- [STRONG] The Great Moon Hoax: the New York *Sun* printed the first of six instalments on 25 August 1835 (four days after the Gulf truce was signed) — [Wikipedia, 1835](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1835)
- [MEDIUM] August 1835: Henry Fox Talbot exposed the first known photographic negatives at Lacock Abbey — [Wikipedia, 1835](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1835)
- [STRONG] Charles Darwin arrived at the Galápagos aboard HMS *Beagle* on 7 September 1835 (the story already used by the Galápagos "1835" café) — [Wikipedia, 1835](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1835)

## B5. Other meanings of 18 and 35, including 18:35 as sunset in Dubai

### Takeaway
18:35 is a real Dubai sunset time, but only on a few days a year: around 1 to 3 April and again around 4 September. Across the year Dubai's sunset ranges from about 17:29 to 19:13, so "18:35 = sunset" is true only as a seasonal moment, not a general fact.

### Cited Findings
- [STRONG] timeanddate.com lists Dubai sunset at 6:35 pm on 1, 2 and 3 April 2027 (6:36 pm on 4 and 5 April; 6:48 pm by 30 April) — [timeanddate.com, Dubai, April 2027](https://www.timeanddate.com/sun/united-arab-emirates/dubai?month=4&year=2027)

### Inferences
- My own calculation with the standard NOAA sunrise/sunset formula (Dubai 25.20°N, 55.27°E, UTC+4; script kept at /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-main-main/a3e411fe-40da-5188-8c1d-341b9d2b82a6/scratchpad/sun.py) reproduces the timeanddate April figures exactly and gives: earliest sunset about 17:29 (around 30 November to early December), latest about 19:13 (early July), and a second 18:35 sunset on about 4 September (18:36 on 3 September, 18:34 on 5 September). The September date and the annual range are computed, not read from timeanddate, and should be spot-checked there.
- 18:35 falls in the pre-sunset golden hour from roughly mid-April to late August (sunset 18:41 to 19:13) and after dark from about mid-September to mid-March. It works as "aperitivo hour" more reliably than as "sunset".
- Ramadan/iftar tie-in: iftar is at sunset, so it would coincide with 18:35 only when Ramadan falls in early April or early September. Not verified against a calendar.

### Gaps
- No sourced numerological meaning of 18 or 35 was researched (e.g. 18 = "chai"/life in Hebrew gematria is widely cited but was not verified here and has no Italian or Gulf relevance).
- Coordinates and addresses: nothing checked. Whether 1835 is a street number, plot number, PO box, or a family date for the owner is unknown; this is the owner's to supply.
